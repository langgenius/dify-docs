# The drafting turn

The standard is the style guide's "What a Good Page Does"; the rest of the guide says how to meet it, and the drafter reads all of it. This file is how a drafting turn holds itself to the standard.

## Open the turn with the reader

Fill this in from the task analysis and put it at the top of the turn that does the drafting, because a drafter who starts from the reader writes toward them:

> I'm writing [page or section] for [the persona, in one clause]. They arrive [what they were just doing] and need [what this text has to give them to keep moving]. With that in mind: [the drafting task].

## Per section, before drafting

One line: the reader's moment and the question they bring to this section. One sentence: the judgment the section owes them, the thing they could not get from the interface or from common sense. Draft from those two lines. A fact enters because that reader would ask for it here.

## Read it back

Before any mechanical check, read the whole draft once as its reader, first time on the page, against the style guide's "What a Good Page Does". Stop at every sentence you would not say aloud to a colleague, every noun phrase doing a verb's work, every sentence carrying two ideas, every fact the reader would not have asked for, every slot kept for a template's sake. Fix those first. The linters measure what is left.
