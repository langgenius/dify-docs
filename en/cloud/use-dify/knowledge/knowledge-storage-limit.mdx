Every workspace has two knowledge base quotas: how many documents it can hold, and how much indexed data its knowledge bases can store. Classic and agent knowledge bases share both. The workspace owner and admins can check usage in **Settings** > **Billing**, under **Documents Upload Quota** and **Knowledge Data Storage**. For each plan's quotas, see the [Dify pricing page](https://dify.ai/pricing).

## Document Quota

The document quota caps the total number of documents across the workspace's knowledge bases, whether you upload them or import them from a source. Every document counts, including ones that are disabled, archived, or failed to process.

At the quota, uploads and imports fail. To add more, delete documents you no longer need or upgrade your plan.

## Knowledge Data Storage

Each document is split into chunks, the passages a search returns. Knowledge data storage holds those chunks in searchable form: their text and their vectors. A vector is the numeric form of a chunk that semantic search matches by meaning. Your original files don't count, so usage depends on how much your documents produce once indexed, not on their file size.

### What Uses Storage

In a classic knowledge base, documents indexed with the **High Quality** [index method](/en/cloud/use-dify/knowledge/create-knowledge/setting-indexing-methods) store a vector for each chunk, or for each child chunk in [Parent-child mode](/en/cloud/use-dify/knowledge/create-knowledge/chunking-and-cleaning-text). Documents indexed with the **Economical** method use keyword indexing instead and take no storage.

In an agent knowledge base, every chunk is stored both as a vector, for matching by meaning, and as text, for matching by keywords. The knowledge graph adds vectors for the subjects it maps and how they connect.

In both kinds, a vision-capable embedding model adds a vector for each image. The embedding model also sets how large each vector is: OpenAI's `text-embedding-3-large`, for example, produces vectors twice the size of `text-embedding-3-small`'s.

The usage figure isn't live and can take several minutes to catch up after an upload or deletion.

### When an Upload Would Exceed the Limit

In a classic knowledge base, an upload estimated to push usage past your plan's limit isn't indexed. The document stays in the list with an **Error** status.

To index it, upgrade your plan or free up storage, then click **Retry** above the document list. Space you free up can take up to 30 minutes to count toward a retry.

You can also delete the document and upload it again with a larger chunk size in the [chunk settings](/en/cloud/use-dify/knowledge/create-knowledge/chunking-and-cleaning-text), so it produces fewer chunks. Larger chunks make each retrieved passage longer and less precise.

### When Storage Is Full

Once usage reaches your plan's limit, neither kind of knowledge base can add content:

- **Classic knowledge bases** block uploading documents; adding or editing chunks; enabling, disabling, archiving, and restoring documents; and re-indexing, such as after changing chunk settings. This holds even in knowledge bases that use **Economical** indexing, because the block covers the whole workspace.
- **Agent knowledge bases** can't process new or changed content. Documents from uploads, imports, syncs, and re-indexing fail to process, and an embedding model change can't run. Searching and deleting keep working.

To lift the block, free up storage or upgrade your plan, then retry any agent knowledge base documents that failed.

If a downgrade or an expired subscription leaves your data above the new plan's limit, nothing is deleted, and your apps can still search these knowledge bases. Adding content stays blocked until usage fits the limit or you upgrade again.

### Free Up Storage

Delete the documents or knowledge bases you no longer need.

An agent knowledge base deletes content in the background, so its space can take several more minutes to return. Before you retry failed documents, check that **Knowledge Data Storage** shows usage below your limit. Otherwise the retry can fail again.
